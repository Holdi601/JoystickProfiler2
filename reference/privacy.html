Privacy

From docs/privacy.md in the repository (generated, always the current text).

JoyPro has no account, no telemetry and no analytics. Your profiles, game configurations, logs and crash reports stay on your PC. A problem report is a zip file that JoyPro only creates when you press "Create Report"; it stays on your PC and you decide whether to send it (see "Problem report" below). User name, e-mail addresses and passwords are removed from it.

Telemetry recording (Settings > Telemetry recording, off by default; telemetry-recording.md) records your games' telemetry into files in a folder you pick. It connects to nothing on the internet: it only listens on 127.0.0.1, reads the games' local files or shared memory and asks the game's own local server (War Thunder), and only while the game runs. Nothing is uploaded. War Thunder's chat is never read; kill messages in a recording name other players, as the game's HUD does. Delete recordings in the same section.

Usage statistics (usage-statistics.md) always run: JoyPro reads which games, aircraft and ships you use from the games' own files on this PC (logs, journals, logbooks, replays, savegames) and from Steam's local playtime file, and keeps a summary in its data folder (<data>\Usage). Nothing is sent anywhere; the Statistics page that shows it is opt-in. Problem reports do not include the summary.

JoyPro connects to the internet only for the things listed below. Each connection shows your IP address to the site it contacts (as any browser visit does). The site's own privacy policy applies to it.

#Problem report (Settings > Report a problem)

"Create Report" saves one zip on your PC with your description and what the developer needs to find a problem, so you are not asked for a second file. It is made only when you press the button, it lists what goes in before it is saved, and it stays on your PC until you send it yourself. JoyPro uploads nothing. It holds:

  • JoyPro's logs (at least the last 14 days, back to the oldest saved version of your open profile, at most 100 MB), errors and crash reports;
  • JoyPro's settings and state (app settings, the game lists and installed packs with their versions, update records);
  • your devices (names, VID:PID, GUIDs) and the vJoy / HidHide state;
  • per installed game: the folders JoyPro uses (drive, free space), JoyPro's backups (the list and the newest one), for MSFS the input profiles JoyPro reads;
  • with the box Include my open profile and its history (your binds and device names) (on by default, remembered): the open profile (only that one), its saved versions (history) and each game's current bind files with JoyPro's files next to them. Untick it to leave these out.

Your user folder becomes %USERPROFILE%, your Windows user name <user> and the PC name <pc>; e-mail addresses, passwords and tokens are removed and Steam ids replaced by a hash, in every text and also inside the copies of the profile and its history. Only the copies in the zip are changed, never your files. Your usage statistics are not in it. You can open the zip and look at everything before you send it. Details: logging.md.

#Device photos (asked per device)

JoyPro ships no product photo. Its photo list (DB\DevicePhotos) holds only the address of each photo on the maker's own web site. When JoyPro recognizes one of your devices and such a photo exists, it asks once for that device, on the Layout page and on the device's card on the Start page:

JoyPro found the product photo of your on <maker's site>. Download it and show it here? (It is only stored on this PC.) Yes · No · Always for all my devices · Never

  • Until you say yes, nothing is downloaded and JoyPro shows its own drawing of the device or the outline of its kind.
  • "Download the reference photo" on the Layout page (the maker's photo a JoyPro line drawing was made from) downloads only on the click and, without a yes for that device, asks this question first.
  • The answer is kept per device model (%LOCALAPPDATA%\JoyPro2\DeviceReference\settings.json). The "Maker's photo of this device" switch on the Layout page changes it for the shown device.
  • Settings > Privacy > "Makers' product photos": Ask for each device (default), Always download or Never, and "Ask again for every device" (forgets the answers). Never also takes back every yes. This replaces the old "Manufacturer photos" switch (off there counts as Never).
  • The engine never downloads a device photo; with Never the GUI asks it not even to name the photo addresses (engine hud devices --no-device-photos).
  • When a connected device's reference photos are not on this PC, the Start page and the Layout page offer them (Download · Not now · Always download · No). Download is the yes for that device. "Download reference photos automatically" (Settings > Privacy, also set by Always download) downloads them in the background for every device that appears; Never switches it off.
  • Besides the maker's own site a photo may come from a listed review site or Wikimedia Commons (DB\DevicePhotos\_sources.json; the picture strip names the site and the licence) and, when the address is dead or the photo changed, from its snapshot in the Wayback Machine (web.archive.org). These sites see your IP address.
  • Downloaded photos are checked against their SHA-256 and kept in <data>\DeviceReference\ReferencePhotos.

#Cockpit photos

The Cockpits page (docs/cockpits.md) links real photos of cockpits. JoyPro ships only their addresses. Before it loads any photo of an aircraft it asks:

JoyPro has photos of the cockpit on . Download them and show them here? (They are only stored on this PC.) Yes · No · Always for cockpits · Never

  • Until you say yes, nothing is downloaded; JoyPro's own drawings and the "Open the page" links (your browser) work without it.
  • The answers are kept with the device photo answers (cockpit:<aircraft>, "Always" / "Never" as cockpit:*). Settings > Privacy > "Makers' product photos: Never" also stops cockpit photos; "Ask again for every device" forgets the cockpit answers too.
  • The engine never downloads a cockpit photo.

Game covers are a separate setting (Settings > Privacy > "Game covers from the internet"), see the table.

Everything else is local:

  • Force feedback telemetry talks to the games on your own PC only (127.0.0.1: DCS and IL-2 over UDP, War Thunder's local web server on port 8111).
  • The local AI model runs on your PC (llama-server on 127.0.0.1); your requests never leave the PC.
  • Game covers are read from your own Steam library first (Steam's cache on this PC); only the missing ones are downloaded (see the table). DCS aircraft pictures are read from your DCS installation.
  • Command lists are built from your installed games on this PC (docs/games/README.md). For War Thunder JoyPro reads the game's packed archives (gui.vromfs.bin, lang.vromfs.bin) by default, read only and offline; Settings > Games turns that off (then JoyPro's own list).
  • Everything JoyPro keeps on this PC is in one folder, %LOCALAPPDATA%\JoyPro2 (logs, game data, backups, profile history, usage statistics, pictures; JOYPRO_DATA_DIR for portable setups), plus the GUI settings in %APPDATA%\JoyPro2 and your profiles. Up to 2.3 part of it was in %LOCALAPPDATA%\JoyPro; 2.4 moves it into JoyPro2 on its first start and leaves a link with the old name for older versions (docs/installs.md "Data folder").
