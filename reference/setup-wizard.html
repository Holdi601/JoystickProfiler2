Setup wizard

From docs/setup-wizard.md in the repository (generated, always the current text).

The Setup wizard sets up your controls for the games you watch in one go. It looks at your connected devices, gives each one a role, makes relations for the important functions of your games' aircraft, binds them to your devices and shows you how it did it. You review everything before your profile changes. It runs without AI: the same choices always give the same plan.

  • GUI: Start page > Your games > Set up my devices automatically..., or Binds > Set up my devices automatically.... User guide: GUI wiki.
  • CLI: engine setup devices | aircraft | plan | apply | render (below; CLI wiki).
  • Code: engine/SetupWizard/*.cs (the planner, plain C# 7.3, compiled into the engine and linked into the GUI), engine/EngineSetupWizard.cs (devices, catalogs, profile, apply, sheets, the CLI), gui/JoyPro.Gui/ViewModels/SetupWizardViewModel.cs, gui/JoyPro.Gui/Views/SetupWizardWindow.axaml.

#The five steps

  1. Games. The games ticked for watching on the Start page (Watch for changes, watchedGames in ui-settings.json) are ticked. Change them here for this setup; the watching itself stays as it is.

  2. Devices and roles. JoyPro recognises your devices with the device catalog (model, kind, hand, grip; see device-reference.md) and proposes a role for each one. Change a role in its list; pick the grip of a base that takes several grips. The box on the right says in words what the plan will do.

  3. Priorities.

    • Your aircraft: the aircraft of your games that the local game lists know (DCS: the modules of your DCS database; other games count as one aircraft). Put them into the priority list: Add as next priority makes a new row, Add as equal puts it into the selected row. Rows can be dragged up and down, aircraft can be dragged onto a row; Up, Down, Equal with above, Own row, Remove do the same with buttons. Aircraft in one row count the same; aircraft you do not list count least.
    • Modifiers: none, 1, 2, 3 or 4, and As few as possible.
    • Mode switch (phase layers): Automatic, Always or Off (auto-assign.md).
    • Like the real aircraft?: the realism slider, 0 to 100 %.

    The choices of this step are kept in the settings, and the next wizard starts with them.

  4. Plan. JoyPro makes the plan (engine setup plan): how many functions it placed, what it could not place, the modifiers it made, the rules it followed and a quality score per aircraft (100 % = every function on its best place).

  5. Review. Per device JoyPro's drawing with a table per control group showing what is bound where (engine setup render), for one aircraft or all of them (relation names), and the list of the device's inputs. Select a function to move it to a free input, swap it with another one or take it out of the plan; select a function under Not placed to put it on a free input. Apply to my profile writes the relations, binds and modifiers in one step: one version in the profile history named "Setup wizard: N relations, M binds" (Undo takes all of it back). Export to my games opens the Export page.

#Roles

Role Proposed for Used by
main-stick the right-hand stick (or HOTAS, yoke) the profile uses most, else the best known one the right hand in every aircraft
heli-stick never proposed; choose it for a second stick (e.g. a force feedback base) the right hand in helicopters
left-stick a left-hand stick (catalog hand, or "L-" / "LEFT" in its name) the left hand in space sims; the throttle side when there is no throttle
throttle a throttle or quadrant the left hand in aircraft (and in helicopters without a collective)
collective a collective the left hand in helicopters
pedals rudder pedals yaw / rudder and toe brakes
panel panels, MFDs, button boxes, DIY boards, unknown devices system switches where their type and label fit
keyboard-joystick Wooting and other keyboards with a joystick mode nothing: an extra, kept for your keyboard binds
virtual vJoy, ViGEm nothing: its binds stay
unused a second stick, throttle or pedals of the same hand (one device per hand), wheels, gamepads nothing

The hands per aircraft class:

  • Right hand: the main stick; helicopters use the heli-stick when there is one.
  • Left hand: helicopters the collective, else the throttle; spaceships the left stick, else the throttle; aircraft the throttle, else the left stick.
  • Collective present: the helicopters' collective (ThrottleLeftNonAB / ThrottleRightNonAB, the collective's buttons) goes on the collective, the aircraft's throttle on the throttle. Relations are split where the two classes use different devices ("[Helicopters]" / "[Fixed wing]").
  • Left stick present: the spaceships' translation goes on it: X = strafe left/right, Y = forward/back, twist = up/down (a mini-stick Y or slider when there is no twist). Its hats and buttons count as the left hand (throttle side), never as the main stick (no pitch on the left stick).
  • No left stick: translation on the throttle's mini-stick and main axis (the knowledge map's positions).
  • Pedals: rudder / yaw and the toe brakes.
  • Panels: a function goes on a panel switch whose printed label names it ("LDG GEAR UP", "ANTI SKID ON"); on/off pairs (gear up/down, master arm on/off, lights on/off ...) go on both positions of one toggle switch.

#How the plan is made

  1. Relations. One relation per function of the knowledge map (DB/Functions: the catalog rows per aircraft and game, plus the function map's actions the catalog does not list, as Auto Group uses them) for the chosen games' aircraft. Display keys and keypads only when the setup has such a device.
    • Your profile first: a command that is already in one of your relations stays there; a relation with a bind on a device of this setup keeps it (it occupies its input for its aircraft). An unbound relation of yours is placed like a new one. --replace-existing lays your bound relations out again (each on its old control when possible). A command you switched off stays off.
    • Realism below 50 %: one relation per function for all aircraft (same function, same button everywhere), split only where aircraft classes use different devices.
    • Realism from 50 %: aircraft whose real HOTAS has the function elsewhere get their own relation (by the side the catalog gives for each aircraft and, with the cockpit database, the exact position). After placing, parts of one function that ended on the same input become one relation again; a function in one piece keeps its plain name.
  2. Importance of a relation = category weight x frequency weight x priority x flight phase weight for the roles of its aircraft x time criticality (auto-assign.md).
    • Category (from the function map's categories, relations functions): flight controls 8 (flight, engine axes), weapons / countermeasures / HOTAS switches / sensors 4, systems (nav, lights, aircraft systems, engine buttons) 2, views and comms 1, display keys 0.5.
    • Frequency (catalog per aircraft): constant 5, frequent 3, occasional 2, rare 1.
    • Priority: row 1 weighs 1, each row below 0.6 of the one above, unlisted aircraft half of the last row; without a list every aircraft weighs 1. A relation counts its highest aircraft plus a little (0.15 each, up to 5) for the others. DCS input layers (Default, NS430, voice) count like the average aircraft.
  3. Score of a relation on an input = importance x (comfort + realism x 2 x real match).
    • Comfort: the function's position preference (slot_controls.tsv, exact position +0.3), its label on a panel (+1.5, a different label -0.6, the label's direction +-0.8), reach (grip > base > panel, more for frequent functions), a modifier layer (minus, more with As few as possible), a latched switch for a non-pair (minus), your own previous control (+4).
    • Real match 0..1, weighted by aircraft priority: the cockpit database's position (exact 1, same hat 0.5, same hand 0.2); without an entry the knowledge map's position on the side this aircraft has the function (0.8 / 0.5 / 0.25).
  4. Constraints. Axis on axis, button on button. Only the devices the hands reach for the relation's aircraft classes. Trigger, weapon release, pinky, detents and flight axes only take functions made for them (or the real aircraft's function there at realism from 50 %). Cockpit panel functions stay off the grips. An input serves several relations only when they have no aircraft in common.
  5. Optimizer. Kept binds first, then your own previous controls, then rounds of on/off pairs on toggle switches, hats as groups (TMS, DMS, CMS, trim ...) and single functions by importance: at realism from 50 % first only real positions, then only strong matches (labels, exact positions, the left stick's translation), then everything. Then local search: move a function to a better free input, swap two, let an unplaced function take the input of a less valuable one that can move elsewhere; passes in a shuffled order with a fixed seed (--seed), so the same choices give the same plan.

#Modifier budget

  • The modifier buttons: your profile's own modifiers first (most used), then the pinky lever / button, the lower index button, the throttle's pinky, other grip buttons; on the main stick before the throttle. Layers copy the grip buttons.
  • As few as possible (--prefer-fewer, default): no modifier while the essential functions (flight controls, weapons, countermeasures, sensors used constantly or often) of the top priority row fit; up to your number when they do not.
  • Otherwise (--use-all-modifiers) your number of layers is offered; a layer nobody uses is not made.
  • Too few inputs: when the essential functions of the top aircraft still do not fit and the hands' grip buttons are fewer than those functions, JoyPro adds modifiers beyond your choice (up to 2) and says so: "Only 6 free buttons on the sticks for the 14 most important functions of F-16C_50: JoyPro added 1 modifier although you asked for none."

#Quality per aircraft

For each aircraft: the importance-weighted average of (score on its input / best score it could get on any input of the setup); kept binds count 100 %, unplaced functions 0 %. Also placed / total and the essential ones.

#Cockpit database

The realism slider uses the cockpit database (another part of JoyPro, engine cockpits get <aircraft> [<variant>] --json) when it is there: per control position of the real HOTAS the function ids there. The planner reads (keys in any case; a list of such entries or { "Cockpit": {...} } work too):

{ "aircraft": "F-16C_50", "variant": "", "game": "DCS",
  "positions": [ { "control": "stick.hat.tms.up", "functions": ["target_management_system_up"] } ] }

control is the device vocabulary (devices.md); functions are the slot ids of DB/Functions/slots.json (function for one id; controls / hotas for positions, position / id for control are accepted). The answer of engine cockpits get <aircraft> --json itself (format joypro-cockpit-layout, cockpits.md) is read as well: its Games[].Plane (else the game) get Devices[].Controls[].Functions[] (Vocabulary -> Fn). cockpits get takes the game's own aircraft name (F-16C_50, FA-18C_hornet) as well as the database's ids.

Sources, in this order: --cockpit-file <json> (the GUI saves the answer of engine cockpits get for each aircraft of the priority list at realism 1 % or more), then the cockpit database itself (DB\Cockpits, read with the engine's CockpitCatalog: every variant that names a game aircraft gives it its positions; a game-wide entry goes to the game's key, first variant only). An aircraft a file gave keeps that entry. Without an entry the knowledge map is used (the plan's notes name the sources). Code: engine/SetupWizard/SetupCockpits.cs (ICockpitPositions, CockpitPositionTable.AddJson / AddCatalog).

#CLI

All print JSON without --text. Device and role options are shared by devices and plan.

  • engine setup devices [--grip <device>=<grip id>]... [--role <device|product|VID:PID>=<role>]... [--add-device <catalog id>]...: devices with Role, ProposedRole, Reason, Inputs, grips, and the Rules in words.
  • engine setup aircraft --games <g1,g2>: the aircraft for the priority list (Key, Game, Class).
  • engine setup plan --games <g1,g2> [--priorities "A>B|C>D"] [--priority "B|C"]... [--modifiers 0..8] [--prefer-fewer|--use-all-modifiers] [--realism 0..1|0..100%] [--seed N] [--replace-existing] [--phase-layers on|off|auto] [--role ...] [--grip ...] [--add-device ...] [--cockpit-file <json>]... [--out-plan <plan.json>]: > separates rows, | (or =) aircraft of one row. Read-only. The plan file has Devices, Rules, Inputs (of the devices with a role that binds), Modifiers (name, input, Forced, reason), Relations (name, function, category, nodes to add, aircraft, Overlap = the aircraft it occupies, device, input, control, label, modifier, score, RealMatch, reason, Kept, Existing, Removed), NotPlaced (with the reason), Quality, Notes, Warnings.
  • engine setup check --plan <plan.json> [--modifiers N]: the rules every plan keeps (C1-C10 of the combination matrix) and its measures; prints JSON, or text with --text.
  • engine setup apply --plan <plan.json> [--dry-run] [--force]: creates or extends the relations (group Setup), sets the binds and modifiers. Refused when two relations with an aircraft in common share an input (the review's edits are checked the same way) unless --force. A command that another relation got in the meantime stays there. One profile save = one history version "Setup wizard: N relations, M binds".
  • engine setup render --plan <plan.json> --out-dir <folder> [--aircraft <name>] [--light] [--width <px>]: a callout sheet (JoyPro's drawing, tables per control group, the layout code of the Layout page) per device with a role that binds; with --aircraft the function names of that aircraft, else the relation names.

#Example: a pit like the author's

Fake device set engine/tests/fixtures/setupwizard/devices.tsv: VKB Gunfighter with MCG Pro, VPforce Rhino (grip not picked), Virpil WarBRD with Alpha-L, VPC MT-50CM throttle, MFG Crosswind V2, WinWing F/A-18 takeoff panel, vJoy, Wooting. Games DCS and Star Citizen, priorities F-16C_50 > FA-18C_hornet = A-10C II > StarCitizen, 1 modifier as few as possible, realism 30 %:

  • Roles: VKB main stick, Alpha-L left stick, MT-50 throttle, Crosswind pedals, takeoff panel panel, Wooting keyboard joystick, vJoy virtual, Rhino not used (a second right-hand stick; choose "Stick for helicopters" to fly helicopters with it).
  • About 360 relations; about 310 functions placed, no modifier needed (the essential functions fit), quality around 85 % for the F-16, F/A-18 and A-10C II.
  • Star Citizen: strafe left/right, forward/back, up/down on the Alpha-L's X, Y and twist; shields, missiles and targets on its hats; pitch and roll on the VKB.
  • The F-16: TMS, DMS and CMS on the VKB's hats, trigger and weapon release on their own, trim on the thumb stick, TDC on the throttle's mini-stick, gear, flaps, anti skid, launch bar and the landing / taxi light on the takeoff panel's labelled switches.
  • Helicopters: no collective, so their collective is on the throttle.
