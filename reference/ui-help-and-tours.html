Help mode, tours and the welcome

Show:

From docs/ui/help-and-tours.md in the repository (generated, always the current text).

JoyPro explains itself over every page: F1's help mode explains any control you click instead of running it, the tours point at the real controls one step at a time, the welcome asks once what the user wants to do and starts the fitting path; its quick answer leads into JoyPro's one quick path (the setup run by itself). Wiki page: website/content/gui/help.md (gui/help.html).

Code:

Part Files
The layer over the window (help catcher, outlines, dims, help popup, tour card, welcome card) gui/JoyPro.Gui/Views/Controls/HelpLayer.axaml (+ .axaml.cs), placed in Views/MainWindow.axaml inside UiScaleHost over RootLayoutGrid
Keys (F1, Esc, Enter, Space) Views/MainWindow.Help.cs
Help mode ViewModels/MainWindowViewModel.Help.cs, Services/Help/HelpResolver.cs, Services/Help/HelpCatalog.cs

The banner at the top (next to Light / Dark / System) has Tour of this page (PageTourButton, StartPageTourCommand) and Help (HelpButton, a menu: Help mode (F1) ToggleHelpModeCommand, Tour of this page, Tours... OpenWelcomeCommand, Open the wiki OpenPageWikiCommand). #Help mode

F1 (no modifier keys) or Help > Help mode (F1) switches IsHelpModeOn on; F1, Esc or End help mode in the banner switch it off. F1 does nothing while the welcome is open; opening the welcome or a tour switches help mode off.

While it is on, the transparent HelpCatcher (help cursor) covers the content, so a click never reaches a control:

  1. HelpLayer.TargetAt hit-tests the content below the point and HelpResolver.FindTarget walks up to the closest help target: Button (also toggle buttons, check boxes, radio buttons), MenuItem, TextBox, ComboBox, Slider, NumericUpDown, ListBoxItem, TabItem, TreeViewItem, Expander, AutoCompleteBox, a Border with the class chip, or one of the custom drawn controls (DeviceMarkerCanvas, AxisCurveView, ...). A part of a control's template is lifted to the control (the arrow of a drop-down list is the list); a scroll bar is no target.
  2. The page: the bars at the top and bottom (ProfileHeaderGrid, BottomBar, BrandBanner) are the profile bar's (header), the Output panel is output, everything else is the open page (HelpCatalog.PageOf(CurrentStage)).
  3. HelpResolver.Resolve finds the control's catalog entry, in this order: the name of its view model command (the command object is compared with the ICommand properties of the DataContexts above it), its x:Name, its shown label and tooltip together, its label, its tooltip. Labels and tooltips are compared in the language shown (the catalog's English label goes through T() first). Entries of the same page win.
  4. Without an entry the text comes from the control itself: the list row's kind (HelpCatalog.RowTexts, by the row's item type), the drawing (DrawingTexts), else its tooltip or placeholder plus what that kind of control does (HelpCatalog.KindWhat). A click on an empty place shows the page (HelpCatalog.Pages).
  5. The popup (HelpTopic): the kind in plain words (a bottom-bar button is a Page button), the title, then the lead: the entry's plain sentence (HelpTopic.Plain, at most 12 words, the wiki's <!-- easy: ... -->), else what it does (sentence 1 of the entry). A button with a MenuFlyout lists its entries. More details / Fewer details (ToggleHelpDetailsCommand, IsHelpDetailsOpen: open by default, closed with easy words) shows or hides the rest: what it does in full, Why it matters (the page's place in the bigger picture), What happens next (sentence 2 of the entry, else the page's next step) and Words explained (at most two JoyPro words of the text, HelpCatalog.Words). More in the wiki (https://holdi601.github.io/JoystickProfiler2/gui/<page>.html#<anchor>, only https URLs are opened). A click on an empty place leads with the page's plain line (HelpPageText.Easy).

The help buttons at the top (Help, Tour of this page, End help mode) explain themselves with the help's own page ("Help and tours"), not the profile bar's. A shared control (a component card on the Virtual Joystick page) is explained with the page it is on. The Virtual Joystick page's tabs have their own lines (HelpResolver.NamedTexts: "Shows the pretend controller moving with your stick ..."), not "Shows another part of this page".

The hover outline shows which control a click would explain; the mouse wheel still scrolls the page under the catcher. With the keyboard, Enter or Space on the focused control shows its help instead of pressing it. The cards are placed next to their control (right, left, below, above, whatever fits first) and always inside the window; they are placed again after every layout change (scroll, resize, UI scale, page change).

#The catalog: the wiki is the source

Every control's text is its wiki entry (<!-- entry: KEY --> in website/content/gui/<page>.md, keys: the command name, bind:<IsChecked property>, click:<handler>, the x:Name, or label:<text>). python website/build.py --help-catalog writes gui/JoyPro.Gui/Resources/help-controls.json (embedded as JoyPro.Gui.HelpControls.json): one row per control with page, key, kind, wiki anchor, command, binding, click handler, name, label, tooltip, w (what it does) and x (what happens next). help_sentences makes plain sentences of the entry (no code, links as their text, no details in parentheses, no engine lines); a first sentence under 40 characters is joined with the next; the second sentence is only taken up to 170 characters. A control without a hand-written entry gets its tooltip or its command's code comment.

So write each entry with a self-contained first sentence (what the control does) and a second sentence with the consequence. A control a novice clicks also gets a plain line right below its entry line: <!-- easy: Click it, then press a button on your stick. --> (catalog field e, at most 12 words a sentence, no address where a language would have to pick formal or informal). website/build.py --check warns on an easy line over 12 words; NoviceTourTests lists the controls that must have one. python website/build.py --check warns, and so fails the gate, while help-controls.json differs from what the wiki makes: run --help-catalog after editing an entry and commit both.

#The welcome

ShouldShowWelcome: the UI settings are kept (persistUiFiles), the welcome was never answered (tours.welcomeDone is false), and the run is not a test or sandbox run (UserFolders.IsSandboxed, i.e. JOYPRO_TEST_SANDBOX) and not started with JOYPRO_NO_WELCOME=1. So everyone who opens a version with the welcome for the first time sees it once, also users coming from an older 2.x (their ui-settings.json has no tours). It is answered by Start (with any answer, also No tour) or Not now; both save welcomeDone, welcomeVersion, choice and everyFeature.

Order at start (MainWindowViewModel.AppServices.cs): after the startup detection, when the first-start game setup shows, the welcome waits until that window is closed (Done or Later, MarkGameSetupDone -> OnGameSetupClosedForWelcome); otherwise it shows at once. When no welcome shows, a tour that was running at the last close goes on at its step.

JoyPro.Gui.exe --welcome (or --tour) opens the welcome on the window's first Opened, also when it was answered. A second start with --welcome finds the running JoyPro (single-instance guard), leaves :welcome (StartupOptions.WelcomeRequest) in its forwarded request list and signals it; the running window comes to the front and opens the welcome (AppShell). Resources/JoyPro 2 - Tour.cmd is copied next to JoyPro.Gui.exe on build and publish (JoyPro.Gui.csproj) and runs start "" "%~dp0JoyPro.Gui.exe" --welcome; tools/publish_release.ps1 fails when the package misses it.

#Tours

A tour is a TourDefinition of TourSteps. A step names its target:

Target Finds
nav:<page> the page's button of the bottom bar (Nav_<page>, nav:output = EngineOutputQuickToggle)
name:<x:Name> the first shown control with that name
cmd:<Command> the first shown button bound to that view model command
tab:<English header> the first shown tab whose header is the translated text
section:<id> the Settings section (the step opens it)
a|b the first of the alternatives that is shown
page the open page (PagesHost)
empty / none nothing: the whole window is dimmed and the card stands in the middle

x>y (name:VirtualJoystickViGEmNeeded>ComponentConsentBox) is the control named y inside the control x finds (the license box of the ViGEmBus card, not of the first component card on the page).

Targets are found by name, command or header, never by position, so window size, UI scale, theme and the user's order and hidden pages of the bottom bar do not matter. Stage opens a page before the step; WaitStage waits until the user opens that page (its bottom-bar button is the target; Next opens the page itself, also a hidden one); WaitClick waits for a click on the target button (Next goes on without it). The layer dims the window around a hole over the target, so the target stays clickable, outlines it and places the card beside it. A target not on screen yet is looked up again on the next layout.

A step never points at nothing. When its target does not show in the user's state, Otherwise lists other targets, each with its own text: the Start page's "Set up automatically" points at Fill the gaps... (or Import, then fill the gaps) when the games already have binds and Set it all up for me is not there. A target in a closed section (an Expander) opens it; Prepare picks what a control needs to show (a game control for Record input, an axis for the curve, a relation for its commands); OpenTab opens its tab; a found target outside the view is scrolled into view (again once it has its layout). When nothing fits after about 0.4 s, the step is left out in the direction the user was going (a hidden page button: the page opens, as Next does). Only none and empty targets stand in the middle on purpose. TourTargetWalkTests walks every step of every tour in an empty profile, a profile with binds in several games and with easy words, and checks that each step outlines a shown control inside the window.

Back, Next (Finish on the last step, the one big blue button), Skip (next step without doing it) and End tour (both small text links) are on the card. A waiting step says so in a coloured box: "Waiting for you to click the glowing button. Or press Next ...". The tour's control glows (TourOutline has a blue shadow) and the rest of the window is dimmed with JP.TourDimBrush (darker than a question's dim, so it shows on a dark page). A step's OpenTab opens that tab of its page; Picture draws a small explainer (TourPictures: axis, pad, export, alttab: the keys Alt + Tab and the game and JoyPro's window with arrows both ways) with a caption above the text. A tour ends finished (last step) or ended (End tour); only a finished tour counts as seen. Skip is hidden on the last step (it would do what Finish does). A tour joined in the middle (the pad steps after the first start's quick path) counts its steps from there ("Step 1 of 6", not "Step 4 of 9"), and Back stops there. The pad steps begin there with "Why this page" ("Your game only knows an Xbox controller. JoyPro turns your stick into a pretend one."), a step left out (TourConditions.FromTheStart) when the tour runs from its first card, which says the same. The step that opens the page points at the glowing bar button instead of naming it (it is "Pretend controller" for a pad-only player, VirtualJoystickNavTitle, else "Virtual Joystick").

#Steps that wait or come in two parts

  • The setup window (TourStep.Waiting / WaitingEasy, the pad tour's TourActions.QuickSetup step): once the quick setup is opened from the card, its Next, or the page's own button (a click on the glowing target), the card stays on its step (TourWaitsForSetup): title "Finish the setup window first", no glow (target none), Next is Show the setup window (brings the window to the front). It never runs ahead (a page change does not move it either). There is no Skip while it waits (Skip would run ahead; the key does what Next does). Set up the pretend controller in the window goes on to the pad steps (ContinueWithPadSteps); the window closed (OnSetupWizardWindowClosed, from MainWindow) with the buttons applied does the same, without them the step shows again.
  • Two parts (ThenWhen, Then, ThenEasy, ThenTarget): once the condition holds, the card shows its second text and points at ThenTarget. TourConditions.Clicked: Test was clicked, the card says to move the stick and waits for Next. TourConditions.ViGEmConsent: the ViGEmBus license box is ticked (here or in the list of components), the card says "now click Install ViGEmBus" and the button glows.
  • A pick in a list (WaitFor: TourConditions.BindSelected): the Binds list glows; a pick goes on by itself, Next picks the first row. Record input shows only with a row picked, so its step has Prepare: TourPrepare.SelectBind (it picks the first row when none is).
  • Warn before skipping (WarnSkip / WarnSkipEasy): the helper step without the helper: the first Next or Skip shows what will not work ("your game will not see your stick"), the second goes on.

Every card has Easier words (Normal words while easy words are on, ToggleEasyWordsCommand), so the switch is there in the moment a card is too hard: the first start's quick path skips the welcome.

Page tours: TourCatalog.PageTour(page) builds "Tour of the page" from the page's HelpCatalog.Pages text (what it is, where it fits), the page's own steps and "What comes next". Tour of this page always shows every step; the list in Settings > Tours uses the section's own Only the essentials / Every feature, Easy words choice (every feature by default).

#What is remembered

%APPDATA%\JoyPro2\ui-settings.json, "tours" (ToursSettings, camelCase like the rest of the file):

Key Meaning
welcomeDone the welcome was answered (it does not show by itself again)
welcomeVersion the JoyPro version that answered it
choice the answer: pad, dcs, quick, new, jp1 or none
everyFeature the welcome's essentials / every feature choice
seen the ids of the tours run to their last step (page:<slug> for page tours)
activeTour, activeStep, activeEveryFeature the tour in progress and its step: it goes on after a restart

Every step change saves at once. Ask again at the next start resets all of it.

#Easy words

One switch for anyone who wants it simple (children included): Settings > Tours (EasyWordsToggle), the welcome (WelcomeEasyWords), the first-start window (GameSetupEasyWords, next to its blue button: its quick path skips the welcome) and Easier words on every tour card, EasyWords, kept in ui-settings.json ("tours" > "easyWords"; Ask again at the next start keeps it). It turns on:

  • the big card style for every tour (TourIsBig), bigger welcome texts;
  • each step's Easy text (and EasyTitle), the welcome's EasyDescriptions and the short suggestion line. They are their own English texts, so a language can write them in the informal address (German "du"); the normal texts keep the formal one ("Sie");
  • F1's help with its details closed (the plain sentence alone, More details opens the rest).

The rules, checked by NoviceTourTests: every step of the pad, quick, new and DCS tours has an easy text, at most 12 words a sentence and 4 sentences, no HidHide / vJoy / mapping / driver / engine; normal card sentences at most 15 words; every page has a plain line; the controls a novice clicks lead with a plain sentence; German easy texts never say "Sie", normal ones never "du" (also the second parts, the waiting cards and the skip warnings); a text shown in both modes and every card title is neutral (infinitive: "Jetzt den Stick bewegen."); UI buttons are "Schaltfläche" in German, "Taste" is a button on the stick.

One name for the virtual Xbox controller in every novice text: EN "pretend controller" (the button Set up the pretend controller, the Virtual Joystick page's title for a player whose games are all on it), DE "Ersatz-Controller" (Ersatz-Controller einrichten); each language keeps the one word its files already used. "Xbox pad" stays only as the name of the mode in the Games list.

#The quick answer

There is one quick path in JoyPro (docs/auto-setup-eval.md): Done, and set everything up for me of the first-start game setup, the Start card's Set it all up for me, and the welcome's quick answer all call StartQuickSetupAsync (Settings > Tours starts the quick tour itself): the games with nothing assigned (else the ones in the profile) go into a setup wizard in QuickMode, which runs by itself (RunQuickAsync: devices, aircraft, plan, review) up to its plain-words review (QuickSummary, QuickNotes for devices JoyPro does not know). Use these buttons applies the plan, Write to my game exports the games (QuickExportAsync); once something was written the setup window closes and the quick tour starts. Games that only know an Xbox controller (TourCatalog.PadGames) get Set up the pretend controller instead (SetupWizardViewModel.PadGamesOnly): the window closes and the pad tour goes on at its first Virtual Joystick step (ContinueWithPadSteps).
