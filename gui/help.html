Help mode and tours

Show:

JoyPro explains itself in four ways. Help mode (F1) turns every click into a question: click a button, field, list row or tab and a small window next to it says what it does, where it fits and what happens next, without running it. Tours point at the real buttons one step at a time and wait for your click; every page has its own Tour of this page. The welcome asks once what you want to do and starts the way that fits you. Its quick answer, "Just make my stick work", starts JoyPro's quick setup: the same one as Done, and set everything up for me of the first start and Set it all up for me on the Start page. Easy words makes all of it as simple as it gets: big cards and the shortest sentences, for children and for anyone who wants it simple.

  1. Your games window
    at the very first start
  2. The welcome
    What do you want to do?
  3. A tour or the quick setup
    whatever fits your answer

Done, and set everything up for me in the Your games window skips the welcome: the quick setup starts at once. Without the Your games window (you update from an older JoyPro 2), the welcome comes first.

For beginners

#Help mode: click anything to learn what it does

Press F1 (or Help at the top right > Help mode (F1)). The mouse pointer turns into a question mark and a line at the top says "Help mode: click anything to see what it does. F1 or Esc ends it."

  • Click a control: it does not run. A small window next to it shows:
    • first one plain sentence of what it does ("Click it, then press a button on your stick."),
    • More details / Fewer details: the longer explanation, Why it matters (what this part of JoyPro is for in the bigger picture), What happens next and Words explained (up to two JoyPro words of the text in one plain sentence each: "Relation: one job, like 'landing gear up', in all your games and aircraft at once."). Open by default; with Easy words it starts closed,
    • More in the wiki: the control's entry on this website.
  • A button with a menu (Help, More ...) lists its entries, each with a short line.
  • An empty place of a page explains the page.
  • The outline under the mouse shows which control a click would explain. The mouse wheel still scrolls the page.
  • With the keyboard: move to a control with Tab and press Enter or Space to see its help.
  • F1, Esc or End help mode in the line at the top ends help mode; clicks run the buttons again.

#The welcome

The first time you open this version of JoyPro (also when you come from an older JoyPro 2), it asks once: What do you want to do? When the "Your games" window of the first start shows, the welcome comes right after it. JoyPro picks the answer that fits the games it found and marks it suggested; pick another if it fits you better.

Answer What happens
Play ACE COMBAT with my flight stick A tour that turns your stick and throttle into a pretend Xbox controller: Set it all up for me first (the quick setup; the card waits while its window is open, and its Set up the pretend controller brings you back), then the Virtual Joystick page: the free helper ViGEmBus when it is missing (first tick the box below the license, then Install ViGEmBus; Windows asks once for permission), Test on the Live test tab (the card stays until you moved your stick and pressed Next), and when to press Start: start the game; at its title screen hold Alt and press Tab to come back, click Start, Alt+Tab back to the game.
DCS World or Falcon BMS A tour of the DCS way: your profile, reading your DCS binds, relations across all aircraft, axis curves, detent mode, export and the check.
Just make my stick work The quick setup (below), then "It worked!" on the Start page and a short tour: where to change a button later (pick a game control in the list, then Record input), how to send it to the game again, "Done! Start your game".
Show me around The five ideas behind JoyPro (profile, relations, binds, export, check), one at a time, with big highlights; it waits for your clicks.
I used JoyPro 1 Where each JoyPro 1 function sits now: load and save, import, relations, binds and curves, export, the validation. JoyPro 2 opens your JoyPro 1 profile as it is: the tour offers Open my JoyPro 1 profile when it finds it (a copy is opened; JoyPro 1's file stays). Suggested when that profile is on the PC.
No tour Nothing starts.

How long a tour? Short shows the steps you need; Long, with every extra also shows the extras (for example the detent step of the DCS tour). Easy words (also in Settings > Tours) makes every card big and its text as short as it gets; German then says "du". Press Start, or Not now to close the welcome. Either way it does not come back by itself; Help > Tours..., Settings > Tours > Show the welcome and the JoyPro 2 - Tour entry next to JoyPro open it again any time.

#Tours

A tour dims the window and makes the real control it talks about glow. A card next to it says what to do; four cards also have a small picture: what a joystick axis is, what the pretend controller is, what Export does, and how Alt+Tab brings you back from a full-screen game.

  • Many steps wait for you: "Click Relations in the bar at the bottom." and "Waiting for you to click the glowing button." The step goes on by itself when you click it. On a step that asks you to open a page, Next opens it for you, even a page you hid in the bottom bar; on a step that waits for another click, Next goes on without it.
  • Next is the big blue button. Back goes one step back; the small links Skip (leaves a step out) and End tour (stops). The last step's Finish ends it.
  • Close JoyPro in the middle of a tour and it goes on at the same step at the next start.
  • Tour of this page (at the top right, next to Help, and in the Help menu) shows the open page: what it is for, its main parts and what comes next.
  • Settings > Tours lists every tour with Start tour; a tour you ran to its end is marked seen.

#The quick setup: "Just make my stick work"

For anyone who wants working buttons and nothing else, the welcome's quick answer opens JoyPro's quick setup (the setup wizard running by itself):

  1. JoyPro finds your games and your devices and makes the setup on its own. A device JoyPro does not know is named in plain words and set up like a usual device of its kind.
  2. The review shows the result as a plain list: which button does what ("Button 4: Reload guns"). Show the picture big shows your device with every button; Change something opens the tools to move, swap or leave out a function.
  3. You press Use these buttons, then Write to my game (only one blue button at a time). The game's old controls are kept as a backup. The window closes and a short tour shows where to change a button later.
  4. For a game that only knows an Xbox controller (ACE COMBAT, Hell Let Loose: Vietnam) the second button is Set up the pretend controller instead: the Virtual Joystick page shows the last steps (ViGEmBus, Test, Start at the title screen). Those games need no export.

When something is in the way (no game found, no stick found), the window says what to do in plain words, with Try again and Set it up step by step (the setup wizard with all its steps).

Advanced

#Details

  • Which answer is suggested: DCS or Falcon BMS installed: DCS World or Falcon BMS. Only Xbox-controller games installed (ACE COMBAT 7, ACE COMBAT 8, Hell Let Loose: Vietnam): Play ACE COMBAT with my flight stick. Anything else, or no game found yet: Just make my stick work. The line above the answers names the games it was made from.
  • The first start's Done, and set everything up for me answers the welcome with the quick way: the setup opens, the welcome does not open next to it.
  • Once per user: the welcome counts as answered after Start or Not now, also for No tour. Settings > Tours > Ask again at the next start brings it back at the next start and marks every tour unseen again.
  • Never on test runs: the welcome does not open when JoyPro runs in its test sandbox or with the environment variable JOYPRO_NO_WELCOME=1.
  • The JoyPro 2 - Tour entry: JoyPro 2 - Tour.cmd next to JoyPro.Gui.exe starts JoyPro.Gui.exe --welcome (also --tour): the welcome opens even when it was answered before. When JoyPro is already running, the running one comes to the front and opens the welcome.
  • Essentials or every feature: the welcome's choice is kept for the path tours; Settings > Tours has its own choice for the tours started from its list. Tour of this page always shows every step.
  • Tours find their controls by name, not by position: your window size, UI scale, theme and the order of the bottom bar do not matter. A control that is not on screen: the card stands in the middle of the dimmed window.
  • F1 does nothing while the welcome is open. Starting a tour or the welcome ends help mode.
  • The quick setup is the one of the first start and the Start page: it runs the setup wizard's steps by itself (engine setup devices, engine setup aircraft, engine setup plan) up to its review; Use these buttons applies the plan (engine setup apply) and Write to my game exports the games like the Export page (with its backup).
In the game: The quick setup writes into the game

The quick setup changes a game only when you press Write to my game: it exports the games, like Export on the Export page. The game's old controls are kept as a backup; Undo export on the Export page puts them back. Help mode and the tours never change your profile or your games by themselves; a tour step only shows the button, you press it.

Technical internals

#Behind the help

  • Layer: Views/Controls/HelpLayer.axaml (+ .axaml.cs) over the window's content, inside the UI scale; keys F1, Esc, Enter and Space: Views/MainWindow.Help.cs. View model: MainWindowViewModel.Help.cs, .Tours.cs; the quick answer calls StartQuickSetupAsync (.AutoSetupStart.cs). The control under a click and its text: Services/Help/HelpResolver.cs; the page texts and the explained words: Services/Help/HelpCatalog.cs; the tours: Services/Help/TourCatalog.cs.
  • The per-control texts are the entries of this wiki: python website/build.py --help-catalog writes the first two sentences of every entry into gui/JoyPro.Gui/Resources/help-controls.json (embedded in the GUI); --check fails while that file is older than the wiki. A control is matched by its command, its name, its label or its tooltip.
  • What the welcome and the tours remember: ui-settings.json, "tours" (answered, the path picked, essentials or every feature, the tours seen, the tour in progress and its step).
  • Start options: Services/StartupOptions.cs (--welcome, --tour), the second start's hand-over: AppShell.cs. Reference: Help mode and tours.
