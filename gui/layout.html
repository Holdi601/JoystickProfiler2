GUI pages

Layout

Show:

The Layout page shows a picture of each of your devices with what every button, hat and axis does. It has two sections, switched at the top: Device pictures (one device at a time: look, fix the markers, bind on the picture) and Cheat sheets (printable pictures of all your devices, per aircraft). Nothing on this page changes a game, except a bind you make in Bind mode, which goes into your profile like any other bind.

For beginners

#Device pictures

  • Left: your devices. One card per stick, throttle, pedals or panel, with its model and "Your layout · 47 markers" or "Reference picture". Click a card to show its picture. Model... says which model it is (if JoyPro guessed wrong); the swap button moves its binds to another device.
  • Middle: the picture. JoyPro's drawing of the device, the maker's photo or your own photo, with a label at every input that says what it does ("Weapon Release", "Gear Up/Down").
  • Right: the selected input (click a label or a row of the legend) and the legend: every input of the device with its bind, "7 of 25 inputs bound".

#The three modes

  • View (V): look at what every input does.
  • Edit (E): drag the labels onto the real buttons, use your own photo, start the marker assistant.
  • Bind (B): click an input, pick a relation on the right, Bind to this input. The bind goes into your profile.

#Cheat sheets

Pictures of your devices with every bind written next to the buttons, to print or to keep on a second screen. Tick the devices, games and aircraft, choose Overview per device and/or One per aircraft, press Create cheat sheets. The pictures (PNG) go into the output folder; the preview shows them.

In the game: cheat sheets and Bind mode

A cheat sheet shows what the buttons do in one aircraft, with that game's own names for the commands when you pick it in the preview box. A bind made in Bind mode is written into the game at the next export, like a bind recorded on the Binds page.

Advanced

#Which picture is shown

  1. Your own layout file (a JoyPro 1 layout, a picture you chose).
  2. Your photo of the device.
  3. The maker's reference photos (one per view), downloaded only after your yes.
  4. JoyPro's drawing of the device.
  5. The outline of its kind (stick, throttle, pedals ...).

The picture strip above the canvas has a button per picture of the device. The box next to the 3D button picks the drawing style: Lines or flat colours under bold outlines (colours in Settings > Drawings). Devices with a 3D model get 3D view: drag to turn, the wheel zooms, a double click resets. The flat drawing is what the cheat sheets print and what Edit mode works on.

#Which model is it?

JoyPro identifies a device by its USB id, its name and the inputs it reports, against a catalog of several hundred models. When it cannot tell (a base with exchangeable grips, one USB id for several products, an unknown device), it asks over the picture: Which one is it? with tiles of the candidates, Most likely first, Keep the most likely, Not listed: search the catalog, Not now. For an unknown device you also pick its kind. Model... > Choose another model... asks again at any time.

#The marker assistant

When the shown picture knows no position of a part ("No positions on this picture yet"), the marker assistant places them in a minute:

  1. Place the markers (or Place them when only a few are missing).
  2. Press a button or hat direction on the device (other devices are ignored), or More > Axis and move an axis.
  3. Click the part's spot on the picture.
  4. Repeat; Finish saves.

Without the device at hand, Now place: X names the next input; a click puts it there, Later asks for it after the others, and the inputs without a place are chips you can drag onto the picture. Undo takes the last placement back; Skip goes to the next input; Stop pauses. More holds Axis, Stop, Add control (a control JoyPro does not know: button, switch, hat, mini-stick, encoder, axis) and Remove from picture.

#Detected spots (experimental)

More > Detected spots (experimental) draws thin dashed rings on the spots of a photo that look like buttons, hats or switches. A click (or a dropped chip) near a ring puts the marker on the ring's centre. JoyPro does not know which input a spot is, so it places nothing by itself; it works on photos, not on JoyPro's drawings, and runs offline.

#Tables and lines

On a picture with known positions, Tables (toolbar) draws JoyPro 1's cheat sheet look: a table per group of controls around the picture, lines to the parts, the bound relation per row and the modifier bind in a second column. The preview box picks what the rows show: Relation names, or one game and aircraft with that game's own menu titles. In Edit mode you drag a table (it gets pinned), its right edge, a line's segment or a spot; the side panel has Modifier column, Row + / Row −, Pinned, Re-route line, Remove line, Split into single tables, Group again, Auto arrange and Reset. A relation dragged from Drag a relation onto a row and dropped on a row binds it there.

#Several views on one page

Several views puts more pictures of one device on a page (front and side, so every button is covered): Add view, Your image..., Arrange, Edit this view, Remove view, Remove page, Back to one picture. The page format is A4 or Letter for printing, 16:9 / 21:9 / 32:9 for a screen. The cheat sheets print the page.

#Correcting JoyPro

When an input is missing or on the wrong spot, fix it in Edit mode (drag, Add control, Remove from picture, rename in the legend). Everything is kept on this PC as your layer over JoyPro's data. Send correction to JoyPro... saves the change as a small file and opens a prefilled issue form on GitHub; you decide there whether to send it. Model... > Reset to JoyPro's default... removes all your changes of a device (asks first; Undo the reset puts them back).

#Keys

V / E / B modes, N place marker (layout file), Del remove, Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Y undo and redo, Ctrl+S save, + / - zoom, 0 or F fit, arrows nudge in Edit mode (Shift: 10 px), Alt drags without snapping, a dropped picture file becomes your photo of the device.
