HUD

Show:

The HUD shows your devices on screen while you play, like a streamer's controller overlay: the stick picture tilts with your stick, levers and axes move as bars, held buttons light up with their names, and a line says what the pressed button does in the running game ("Gear Toggle"). It can show over the game, in OBS, or both, each with its own widgets. This page is its designer.

For beginners

#Start the HUD in three clicks

  1. Open HUD in the bottom bar. The preview already moves when you move your stick.
  2. Leave On my screen (over the game) ticked.
  3. Press Start HUD (also on the Start page). Start your game: the HUD sits on top of it and never takes the mouse or the keyboard away from the game.

Stop HUD (the same button) closes it. Everything you set here is kept and is there again after a restart.

#What is on the screen

  • One widget per device: sticks, throttles and pedals are shown at the start, panels and keyboards are hidden (tick them in the Widgets list). Each widget can show the device's photo, the stick position, the axes as bars, the buttons and hats.
  • The aircraft widget: the stick and throttle of the aircraft you fly, as the game draws them, moving with yours.
  • The game header: the game or DCS aircraft with its picture.
  • What the button does: the last pressed buttons with their action in the running game.

Drag a widget in the preview to move it; click it to change it on the right. Arrange automatically puts them along the bottom: throttle and left-hand devices left, pedals in the middle, the right-hand stick right.

In the game: What you see in the game

The HUD window is transparent and stays on top of windowed and borderless games. Clicks go through it to the game. It is not in the task bar or Alt+Tab. Exclusive full screen hides it (a Windows limit): switch the game to borderless. The pressed button's line names the action of that game and aircraft, for example "Weapon Release" in the F-16C and "Fire guns" in IL-2.

Advanced

#Where the HUD shows

The three outputs are independent of each other:

Output For How
On my screen (over the game) you a transparent window on top of the game
Page for OBS (Browser Source, transparent) your stream (recommended) JoyPro serves a page on this PC only; Copy puts its address on the clipboard
Stream window (Window Capture) streams with Window Capture a normal window (may sit behind the game) with a transparent or chroma key background

Untick On my screen to play with a clean screen while the stream still shows the HUD, or show other widgets on each: every widget has two ticks, a monitor (your screen) and OBS (the stream). Preview: My screen / The stream switches what the designer shows. Updates per second at most (15, 30 or 60; 30 is smooth and light).

#Set up OBS (Browser Source)

  1. Tick Page for OBS (Browser Source, transparent) and press Start HUD.
  2. Press Copy next to the address (http://127.0.0.1:8765/hud).
  3. In OBS: Sources > + > Browser. Paste the address as URL, Width 1920, Height 1080. Nothing else is needed: the background is transparent, no custom CSS.
  4. Optional: tick Shutdown source when not visible so OBS stops the page in scenes without it.

The page draws the widgets ticked for the stream, in the same places and styles as the app, on a 1920 x 1080 stage scaled to the source. Port taken by another program? Pick another one under Port and copy the address again.

Window Capture instead: tick Stream window, pick the background (transparent, green, magenta, blue or your colour). In OBS add Window Capture with Capture Method: Windows 10 (1903 and up); with a colour add a Chroma Key filter in that colour. If a transparent background shows black in your OBS, use the Browser Source or a colour.

#Widgets

Widgets lists every device plus the header, the binds ticker and the aircraft widget. Reload devices reads the connected devices again; Show all ticks every widget for your screen; Arrange automatically and Reset places put them in place.

Per widget (click it in the preview or the list):

  • Show on my screen, Show in the stream, Size, Style of this widget (the HUD's style or any other).
  • Show: name, kind (stick, throttle), picture, picture tilts with the stick, buttons light up, held buttons by name, axes and levers, axis names, axis values, stick position (gimbal), hats. The header widget: its picture.
  • Picture: Photo (the manufacturer's picture, background cut away), Layout (your JoyPro layout picture from the Layout page, with the pressed buttons lit at their place), Auto (the photo when there is one).
  • Grip on this base: for bases that take several grips (VPforce Rhino, Virpil WarBRD); the grip decides the picture and the button names.
  • Stick axes: where X, Y and Twist of the stick element come from. Automatic takes the device's own axes; a grip that is a device of its own without axes takes them from the base it sits on.

The aircraft widget: stick, throttle, throttle travel bar, stick position, controls light up, held controls by name; Aircraft (Automatic: the aircraft you fly, or any picture set on this PC) and Moves with (the stick and throttle that move it). Its pictures come from the game's own cockpit pictures (Cockpits > Build pictures from my games, or a picture pack), else JoyPro's line drawing of the real grip.

#The 40 styles

Style has 40 presets: Glass, Neon, Minimal, clean ones (Frosted light, Streamer clean, Streamer bold, Compact, Large text, Pastel, Outline only), cockpit displays (Military green HUD, Amber MFD, Airliner PFD, Glass cockpit, Night vision, Red cockpit lighting, Vector wireframe, Retro terminal, Warbird, Hologram), games and genres (Space sim orange, Holo cyan, Cyberpunk, Synthwave, Neon pink, Racing, Tactical tan, Navy and gold, Stealth, Ghost), colour themes (Arctic, Desert, Ocean, Sunset, Royal, Carbon) and readability (High contrast, High contrast light, Monochrome, Monochrome light). › steps to the next one.

Below: accent colours in one click, Accent, Pressed and Opacity, then the design options per element: Cards (background, border, corners, shadow), Text (font, size, letter spacing, colours, Bold names), Stick element (ring, square, crosshair, radar, dot; size, glow), Bars and levers (shapes, heights, the empty part) and Buttons and chips (shapes). A changed option makes the style "own design (changed)": Save style keeps it under your name, Undo changes goes back to the preset, Export style... / Import style... share it as a file, Delete removes your own style.

#What the button does and the DCS aircraft

What the button does: how many lines, how long they stay (seconds), the font size, Show device and button and Only while a game runs. Click-through lets mouse clicks pass through the HUD window (applies when the HUD starts).

JoyPro finds the running game by its process (DCS, IL-2, Star Citizen and every other game it handles) and shows only that game's binds. DCS also needs the aircraft: pick it under DCS aircraft, or Install DCS hook so DCS tells JoyPro the aircraft at every mission start (a small Lua file plus one guarded line in Export.lua, backed up first; Remove puts the file back byte for byte; close DCS first). Check says whether the hook is installed.

Technical internals

#Behind the page

View model: MainWindowViewModel.Hud.cs (devices, widgets, layout), MainWindowViewModel.HudStyles.cs (presets, own styles, import/export), MainWindowViewModel.HudStream.cs (outputs, stream page, aircraft widget), MainWindowViewModel.HudAxes.cs (stick axes), MainWindowViewModel.Overlay.cs (start/stop, running game, button lines, DCS hook). Views: Views/HudWindow.axaml, Views/Controls/HudSurface.axaml, HudElements.cs, HudPanel.cs. Presets: Models/HudStylePresets.cs.

  • Devices: engine hud devices gives each connected device's model, kind, hand, product picture, axes with their role, buttons with their names and control ids (Controls, for the aircraft widget) and the layout picture with button positions.
  • Binds of the running game: engine overlay map (--game, --plane, --unit): the bound buttons with modifiers and titles. A bind with modifiers shows while its modifiers are held; the bind with the most matching modifiers wins.
  • DCS hook: engine overlay dcs-hook status / install / remove (--path, --dry-run): Scripts\JoyProOverlay.lua plus one pcall-wrapped line in Export.lua; DCS sends the unit type to 127.0.0.1:1992 (UDP). The hook calls the export functions of other scripts (SRS, Tacview) it replaces.
  • Stream server: Services/HudStreamServer.cs, HudStreamHub.cs, Resources/hud-stream.html; bound to 127.0.0.1 only, requests with another host name are refused. GET /hud (page), /hud/events (Server-Sent Events: layout, state), /hud/img/<id> (pictures as PNG), /hud/state (JSON). Without a connected page it only listens.
  • Cost while you play: Services/HudInputPump.cs keeps only the newest report per device and applies them at the chosen rate; elements redraw only on change; nothing is allocated per report; pictures are cut once (aircraft grips 480 px high). Measured by the HUD soak test against CPU, allocation and memory budgets.
  • Settings: %APPDATA%\JoyPro2\hud.json (places, sizes, per-widget ticks for screen and stream, the style with every option, own styles, outputs, port, background, update rate). Style files: *.joypro-hudstyle.json ("format": "joypro-hud-style").
